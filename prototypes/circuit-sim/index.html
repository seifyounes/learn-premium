Can an agent turn a lecture circuit figure into a correct live simulation?

Four figures. For each one, a fresh agent saw only the figure and the problem statement (no answer key) and wrote a SPICE netlist, a CircuitJS circuit, and for the logic one a DigitalJS circuit. Then independent gates ran every file in ngspice-WASM, headless DigitalJS, and CircuitJS in a real browser, and checked both the wiring and the numbers against the professor's worked solutions.

What this settles, and what it doesn't

Findings

  • First-pass accuracy: 4/4 circuits, 8/8 files. Every netlist and CircuitJS file was right on the first try, with no repair loop. Each agent took about 5 minutes and about 110k tokens, mostly spent learning the CircuitJS text format from its source code. A bundled format reference would cut most of that.
  • A broken CircuitJS circuit still runs, with no error. I moved one resistor end 16 px off its rail. The sim kept running and showed Vo = 43 V. The number check and the net check both caught it. So the gates are mandatory, not optional.
  • The sim caught a slip in the professor's solution. Sheet 5 Q1(i) gives VCEQ = 17.95 V, but its own equation gives 13.95 V. Both engines show 13.94 V. A live sim will visibly disagree with the sheet, so v2 needs a rule for that (v1 showed the sheet's value with a note).
  • The sim and the lecture use different idealisations. A real diode or SCR drop, VT = 25.85 mV vs the course's 25 mV, and bypass capacitors that aren't perfect shorts all move the numbers by 1–5%. The agents predicted every one of these gaps in their own notes. Setting the SPICE temperature to 17 °C (VT = 25 mV) cut the amplifier gap from 5% to 1.6%.
  • A live sim must open at steady state. The buck spent about 38 s of wall-clock ringing before it settled. Seeding the inductor current and capacitor voltage fixes this (the buck shown here starts settled), and ngspice can supply those seed values.
  • The engines complement each other. ngspice (MIT/BSD) is the accurate engine and runs in the browser: the α slider below re-solves a 100 ms transient on every change. CircuitJS (GPL) is the one a student can play with: animated current, switches, and scopes.
  • Not tested: board photos or handwritten figures (all four were clean drawings), op-amps (v1 has none), crowded multi-stage circuits, and a real logic-course figure (the full adder is a synthetic fixture I drew).
