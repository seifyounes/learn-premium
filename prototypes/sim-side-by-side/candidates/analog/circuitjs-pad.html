For the Owner

  • Real: the simulation is stock CircuitJS (self-hosted, same origin) with its sidebar, menus and info box hidden, editing off, the white-background mode, and the canvas multiplied onto the pad sheet so the grid shows through. Its voltage colours and current dots are its own. The sliders and the toggle sit outside the canvas: each change edits the circuit text (duty cycle, load R, firing phase, β, RC, and the re-seeded L and C) and re-imports it through CircuitJS1.importCircuit.
  • Readouts: measured live from CircuitJS through its API on every time step (node voltages and element currents), averaged over whole periods after a settling window. Zo and r′e are not measurable this way and show a dash.
  • Idealisation: CircuitJS has no ideal diode or SCR, so "Professor's ideals" redefines its default junction to a near-ideal one (about 0.07 V at 1 A) for the buck diode and the SCR; "Real devices" restores CircuitJS's own junction (about 0.8–0.9 V). For the CE amp the transistor stays the file's model (VBE = 0.70 V); CircuitJS fixes VT at 25.9 mV, so its gain stays about 5% under the course's r′e = 25 mV / IE key. "Real" adds an assumed 100 V Early voltage.
  • Accuracy: measured live in headless Chrome at the example values against circuits/expected.json. Ideals: buck Vo 19.97 V (key 20), SCR Vo,av 40.50 V (40.5), CE Q-point within 0.3%, Av −154.8 (−163.3, the VT gap above). Real: buck 19.51 V, SCR 40.21 V. At α = 90° the SCR read 26.96 V against the formula's 27.01 V.
  • Weight: measured on a cold load in headless Chrome: 1.34 MB transferred in 41 requests (CircuitJS itself is about 1.1 MB of it), fonts included.
  • Touch: the pad controls are 44 px and work by touch; the canvas is display-only. On a phone CircuitJS still draws its own menu button and a red padlock (its "not editable" mark), and squeezes its two scopes into a narrow strip.
  • To ship: a new circuit needs its text hand-edited for each slider (token positions differ per element type), CircuitJS runs slower than real time (the buck shows about 1 ms of circuit time per second), and its look cannot be pushed further than colours and blending.
