Hold a button, or tap its contact in the ladder. STOP is wired normally closed, so its input bit reads 1 until you press it.

For the Owner

  • Real: every lamp, wire and belt is driven by a PLC scan engine (pad-native-ladder.engine.mjs, 10 ms scan, IEC TON) solving program.json. The same engine replays the answer key in Node: check-pad-native-ladder.mjs, 46/46 PASS. The red 5.00 s dimension is measured from the recorded edges, not typed in.
  • Animated, not simulated: the power trace draws in over about 0.2 s so the eye can follow it; the engine state it shows changed in one scan. Parcels are a visual plant model (belts move only while their motor output is 1); they do not feed back into the PLC.
  • First load: 192 KB over 9 requests (fresh cache, uncompressed local server); 140 KB of that is the pad's three web fonts, the simulator itself is 44 KB. No framework, no third-party script.
  • Touch: push buttons and the START/STOP contacts in the ladder use pointer events with press-and-hold (targets 48 px and up); a quick tap still lasts at least one scan. On phones the buttons stay pinned to the bottom of the screen. Checked with mouse and an emulated 375 px touch viewport, not on a real phone.
  • To ship: a general ladder layout for wider programs (branches inside branches, more function blocks: TOF, TP, CTU/CTD, SET/RESET coils), an editor if students must write rungs, and the agent step that writes program.json from a lecture figure.
