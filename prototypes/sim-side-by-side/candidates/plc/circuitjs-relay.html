The same logic, wired
  1. STOP (NC push button) and START (NO push button) feed relay coil K1; K1's own NO contact across START is the seal-in.
  2. A second K1 contact lights lamp M1 (conveyor 1).
  3. A third K1 contact energises KT, an on-delay relay set to 5 s; KT's contact lights lamp M2 (conveyor 2).
How to drive it
  1. Press and hold START briefly (click or touch the switch), then let go: K1 holds itself in, M1 lights.
  2. About 5 s later M2 lights.
  3. Press STOP: K1 drops, KT drops, both lamps go out.

For the Owner

  • Real vs faked: an electrical simulation (MNA solver) of a relay circuit, not a PLC: no scan cycle, no program. The circuit text (circuitjs-relay.txt) was written by the agent using CircuitJS's own relay coil (on-delay type for KT), relay contact and momentary switch elements. Sim speed is set so 1 s of circuit time takes about 1 s of wall time.
  • Accuracy: check-circuitjs-relay.mjs presses START and STOP with the mouse in headless Chrome and logs every time step: 5/5 PASS. M1 lights 10 ms after K1 pulls in, M2 lights 5.010 s after M1 (5 s delay plus the contact's 5 ms), STOP drops K1, M1 and M2 within 20 ms. Circuit time runs close to wall time (0.95x to 1.06x across runs).
  • First load: 1.30 MB over 36 requests (uncompressed local server; CircuitJS itself is about 1.15 MB of that).
  • Touch: CircuitJS handles touch on its canvas and a normal tap on START is long enough (K1 pulls in within 5 ms), but on a phone the whole ladder shrinks to fit and the switches become small targets. Not tried on a real phone.
  • To ship: good for the hardwired relay-control lectures only; it cannot show rungs, a scan or a TON's ET. Lamps stand in for motors.
