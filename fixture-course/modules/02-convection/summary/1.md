---
title: A surface hands heat to the fluid around it
provenance:
  stated: ['1']
---

A surface warmer than the fluid flowing past it loses heat at a rate set by Newton's law of
cooling:

$$
\dot{Q} = h A \left(T_s - T_\infty\right)
$$

The heat transfer coefficient $h$ grows with the flow: still air gives a small $h$, a fan or a
pumped liquid a larger one. As a resistance, convection is $R = \frac{1}{h A}$, so it adds in
series with the wall it sits on.
