---
title: A wall resists heat like a resistor resists current
---

In steady conduction through a plane wall, the heat rate is set by the temperature drop across
the wall and by the wall's thermal resistance:

$$
\dot{Q} = \frac{\Delta T}{R}, \qquad R = \frac{L}{k A}
$$

A thicker wall (larger $L$) resists more; a better conductor (larger $k$) or a larger face
(larger $A$) resists less. Layers in series add their resistances, $R_\text{total} = \sum R_i$,
and the same $\dot{Q}$ passes through every layer.
